Structured data can describe a service page to search engines, but it cannot explain an unclear offer to a person. Before asking whether a page needs more schema, check whether a prospective customer can understand what you do and whether the page supports the details you want to mark up.

## Start with the page, not the markup

Read the page as someone comparing providers. Can you quickly tell what the service is, who it is for, what the work includes, and what to do next? Are important limits or qualifications clear? If those answers are hard to find, improve the visible page first.

[Google's structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) say markup should represent the page's visible content accurately. It should not describe information that is hidden, irrelevant, or misleading. A technically valid block of markup does not make unsupported statements appropriate.

## Signs the content is the real problem

The page may need content work when it uses broad phrases such as “full-service solutions” without explaining the actual service. Other clues include:

- The page lists features but not the customer need they address.
- The intended audience or service area is unclear.
- Buyers' common questions are unanswered.
- Proof, process, or next steps are missing or difficult to find.
- The page makes claims the business cannot substantiate.

These are communication gaps. Adding schema does not fill them in for visitors.

## Signs the structured data may need review

Schema deserves a closer look when the visible page is specific and accurate, but the markup names a different service, uses stale business details, points to the wrong URL, or describes content that no longer appears. A validation tool may also report syntax or required-property issues for a particular search feature.

Treat tool output as a diagnostic. Passing a test does not promise a special search display, and warnings do not all carry the same meaning. Check what the page says and what the selected markup type is intended to describe.

## A simple decision path

1. **Can a buyer explain the service after reading the page?** If not, clarify the page first.
2. **Are the business and service details current and supported on the page?** Correct the source content before changing markup.
3. **Does the markup describe this page's actual subject?** If not, review the type and properties with whoever maintains the site.
4. **Does a validator identify a specific technical issue?** Use the relevant documentation to decide whether it applies, then retest after a focused fix.

The page and its structured data have different jobs, but they should agree. Clear visible information gives both people and maintainers a sound basis for deciding what markup is appropriate.

## Before you ask for technical help

Share the page URL, the visible details that should be represented, the test or report where you saw a problem, and any recent content changes. That gives a developer something concrete to compare. Avoid asking for “more schema” as a goal by itself; first identify what is unclear or inaccurate.
