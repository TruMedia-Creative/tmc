Structured data can become stale when the website or business changes. A redesign, new service, updated contact details, or removed FAQ may leave markup describing a page that no longer exists. Include schema in the normal website-maintenance process so it stays aligned with public information, as described in [Google's general structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).

## Website changes that should trigger a review

Review relevant pages when you:

- Add, remove, or rename a service.
- Rewrite a page or change its main purpose.
- Add or remove an article, video, or visible FAQ.
- Change a page URL, navigation, or canonical address.
- Redesign a template that generates structured data.

You do not need to rebuild every structured data block after every copy edit. Check whether the changed content affects what the markup describes.

## Business detail changes need extra care

Changes to the business name, contact information, physical location, service area, hours, team, or official profiles may affect multiple pages. Confirm the authoritative details with the business owner, then identify every template or page where those details appear. Don't infer a new office or service area from a campaign page.

## After a redesign or page rewrite

Check that page URLs and canonical links still point to the right destinations, image references still work, and template-generated markup matches the new page structure. If content was removed, remove any markup that still describes it. Test representative pages after template changes, then check the rest if the same template powers them.

## Decide who owns the updates

Small teams can make this manageable with a short handoff checklist:

1. Who approves business facts such as location and hours?
2. Who updates the visible page copy?
3. Who maintains template-generated structured data?
4. Who checks key pages after a release?
5. Where are validation results and follow-up tasks recorded?

Clear ownership helps prevent stale details from lingering after a change. A periodic check is also useful for pages that rarely get edited but include time-sensitive details.
