There is no single schema checklist that every service business should copy. A small, accurate set of structured data that describes the pages you actually have is more useful than adding every type you have heard about.

Start with the job of each page, then check the current [Google documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery) for any search feature you hope to qualify for. Schema.org defines a shared vocabulary; Google Search supports particular types and applies its own [general guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies). Those are related, but not identical, questions.

## Choose schema based on the page's job

### Company and organization details

An organization description can identify the business behind a website. Use the real public name, official URL, and other verified details. Keep that information consistent with the website and the business's authoritative profiles. Do not guess at a legal name, address, logo, or social account.

### Service pages

A service page should first explain the service in visible copy. If you use a service-related type, make sure it represents the actual offering and is tied to the correct business and page. A page title alone is not enough support for details that the page never explains.

### Local and location information

Location markup is relevant when the business has a real location or serves a clearly described area. Distinguish a staffed customer-facing location from a service area. Do not turn every town served into a physical office, and keep hours and contact information current.

### Articles, videos, and FAQs

Article or video markup can describe an article or video that is genuinely present on the page. FAQ content should be visible and useful to visitors before considering any markup. Search feature eligibility and display rules can change, so verify the current feature documentation instead of assuming a type will produce a particular result.

## What not to mark up

Do not add fake reviews, hidden FAQs, unsupported offers, stale details, or information about a different page. Do not add a type merely because a tool accepts it. Google's [general guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) emphasize that structured data should accurately represent visible page content; passing a syntax test does not establish that every quality guideline is met.

## Keep the set small and accurate

Make a simple inventory: page URL, page purpose, facts shown to visitors, and the structured data that describes those facts. Have the person responsible for the website confirm business details and have a developer review implementation questions. Revisit the inventory when pages or business facts change.

The right set depends on the site. For a service business, the first useful step is usually making the organization and service pages accurate and easy to understand, then adding only the markup that fits those pages and current documentation.
